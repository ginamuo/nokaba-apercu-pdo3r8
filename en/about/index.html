The Nokaba architect

About

An unusual path, and a studio that could only have grown from it.

Who I am

MMy name is Gina Oum. I’m a web architect, author and editor. I’m Franco-Canadian, originally from France, settled in New Brunswick for three years now, after ten years in Quebec. Nokaba is my studio, the workshop where I design and build the digital presence of the people I work with.

My path

A life I chose

I didn’t follow the usual salaried path. After my first university degrees, I chose to give my family my time first, and I kept creating, writing and producing on my own terms. I own that choice fully, against the current. It comes from values I hold close, and it has shaped everything I deliver today.

Through all those years, I never stopped learning. No employer, no hierarchy, no imposed rhythm: I set my own bar, and I believe it sits higher than most workplaces ask for. Every part of my life and my career I took on by choice, and I saw it through out of conviction rather than obligation.

What I studied

  • International Business(Germany)
  • ManagementBusiness and public administration (France)
  • CommunicationCommunication studies, major in advertising (France)
  • TheologyThe most recent of the four (Quebec, Canada)

My university training rests on four complementary degrees: International Business, business and public administration, communication with a major in advertising, and, more recently, theology.

One curiosity ran through all of them: how organizations work, how they grow at home and abroad, how they speak to the people they serve, and how they build their work around what they truly value. That cross-disciplinary base still shapes how I approach a project, always tying together strategy, voice, structure, values and purpose.

To that I added years of self-taught study in building websites, first with WordPress and Divi, then with the modern tools I rely on now: Astro, TinaCMS, Supabase, Stripe, Cloudflare, GitHub and more. Today I design, develop and deploy professional-grade sites end to end.

What I’ve built

  • 300,000monthly visitors on Afroplurielles
  • 15 yearsof personal blogging
  • 9 yearsleading a church’s communications
  • 8 yearsat the head of a women’s ministry
  • In progressInternational digital platform and book publishing (in development)

Over the years, I founded and ran several digital platforms. My magazine Afroplurielles, launched in France in the two thousands, grew to three hundred thousand monthly visitors and led me to work with brands like L’Oréal Paris and Maybelline. I also kept a personal blog alive for fifteen years, and its editorial thread continues today in a far wider form that will surface soon.

For nine years, I led communications for a francophone church in Quebec that grew to three hundred members. I designed and produced print pieces, video communications, event posters, editorial content, and four successive versions of the website, rebuilt roughly every two years to keep pace with the technology and the community’s needs.

There I also founded and led a women’s ministry for eight years. It started from nothing and drew more than a hundred women into teaching programs, retreats and conferences that I designed and led myself. I coordinated the team I headed, managed budgets and large-scale events, walked alongside members in their spiritual lives, and grew an active community of around two hundred women day by day. That work taught me to run a living organization, with its many expectations, its stages of growth, and its editorial, human and logistical demands.

Out of all of it, something gradually took shape. Under one roof I gathered what most professionals develop separately: strategic thinking, editorial writing, graphic design, event management, technical development, training and support for people. This range is nothing theoretical. It comes from projects and experiences I carried out and lived from the first detail to the last, each one feeding the others.

Why Nokaba

Starting a communications and web design studio was the logical end of my whole path, the formal setup of a practice that already existed. Nokaba wasn’t invented. It surfaced, the plain name for work I had been doing all along.

What I bring, a salaried firm would struggle to offer. A conventional role rarely combines writing, strategy, design and technical craft this fully in one person. That rarity is what I now put at my clients’ service: nonprofits, businesses, professionals and organizations who keep our beautiful corner of the country alive.

They deserve a quality digital presence without giving up their local roots, their values or their voice. Nokaba is my answer to a real need to exist in a digital landscape no one can sidestep anymore. It’s a human-scale workshop that gives its time and attention to the economic life of its community, a few projects at a time.
