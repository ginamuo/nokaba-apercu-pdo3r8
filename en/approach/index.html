The method

My approach

Six steps, from our first conversation to the daily life of your project.

The idea

A communications project is a collaboration, not a transaction.

Here is how we will work together, from our first conversation through to the everyday life of your site or your communications.

Every step earns its place. Each one lets you move forward with a clear head, no unpleasant surprises, and no commitment beyond what you actually want.

The path

The six steps

Each one closes with your sign-off before the next begins.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    If you like, I open a private three-day pass to the Belhavre site that best matches your line of work. No entity in the town fits your trade yet? I hand you the nearest one instead, and its mechanics and finish carry straight over to yours.

    You explore freely, try the features, and judge the work I deliver. This step commits you to nothing. It simply tells you who you are dealing with and what you can expect.

    Most misunderstandings in this business trace back to expectations that were never set straight. Discovery heads them off from the very start.

  2. 02

    Listening

    Once you have seen Belhavre, we book a first conversation, over video or in person if you are in the region. This moment sets the foundation.

    I ask about your trade, your values, your audience, your products or services, your competitors, your budget and timeline, and your ambitions. I listen far more than I speak. You tell me about your project, and I sharpen it with you.

    In the days that follow, I send you a quote with the scope, the schedule, and the proposed fees. This step still comes with no strings attached.

    Should your project call for upfront analysis or a detailed brief, that work becomes a separate mandate, billed in proportion to what it delivers.

  3. 03

    Design

    Once the proposal is accepted, I prepare a full working direction for you. Palette, typography, editorial voice, page structure, sample layouts for a web project. For a consulting mandate: positioning, strategic angles, editorial structure, production plan.

    You approve the big choices before I build a single thing. This is where your project takes on its identity. Nothing is imposed. Everything is discussed, adjusted, and signed off with a clear understanding of why.

  4. 04

    Build

    I build your project week by week, true to the direction you approved. You watch the work advance in real time through a private address I share with you.

    At milestones we set in advance, I hand you working versions to gather your adjustments. This steady back and forth rules out nasty surprises and makes the final delivery match what you had in mind.

    This phase asks for your availability, for the exchanges and the sign-offs. How long it takes depends directly on how quickly you respond.

  5. 05

    Handover

    With the project ready, I put in place what we agreed and hand you the keys. For a site, that covers setting up your hosting, your domain name, your professional email, and your forms, all on your own accounts. For a consulting mandate, the final deliverables and support as you roll them out across your teams.

    I then train you to run your site or your strategy on your own. A training session over video or in person, whichever you prefer, with the video sessions recorded for later review, and a written guide tailored to your project.

    You walk away owning everything, with no technical dependence and no lock-in. If you would rather leave the upkeep to me, a dedicated plan can take over from this point.

  6. 06

    Support

    A site or a communications strategy is a living companion that keeps growing with your organization, never a finished object delivered once and left behind.

    I stay your point of contact for adjustments and future changes. Depending on what you need, this work happens on request, or within the maintenance plan you chose at handover.

The constants

What never changes

  • One person, start to finish

    From the first conversation to the handover, you speak to the same person.

  • A schedule set in advance

    The dates are fixed at the outset, and I hold to them.

  • Fees agreed upfront

    The amount is settled before we begin, with no surprise charges at the end.

  • No jargon

    Our exchanges do without technical vocabulary, and where it comes up, I explain it.
